Slashbooks vs outsourced bookkeeping

A qualitative comparison for cash-basis, owner-operated businesses. This covers services like Bench and similar outsourced bookkeeping — without competitor pricing or performance claims.

I was tired of paying for QuickBooks and an outsourced bookkeeper for books that were always behind, so I built a bookkeeper my AI agent runs and that I control.

At a glance

Outsourced bookkeeping Slashbooks
Who keeps the books Humans at a service categorize transactions, reconcile accounts, and prepare reports on a schedule. An AI agent you control, backed by deterministic software. You make judgment calls; the agent handles repetitive work.
How current are the books Reports often lag the month they cover. Back-and-forth on unfamiliar transactions slows things down. Run the monthly close on a schedule and have current books waiting. Ask questions anytime with every figure already at hand.
Who knows your business Bookkeepers learn your patterns over time, but they don't know your business as well as you do. You confirm or correct unfamiliar transactions. Repeated confirmations teach the system your categories; corrections reset trust when something was wrong.
Where the books live Typically inside the service's workflow and whatever accounting software they use on your behalf. Local files in a folder you choose. ledger.sqlite, review queue, audit log, and reports — all on your machine.
Accountant's role Your accountant reviews the books the service prepares. Your accountant still reviews the output. Slashbooks generates Excel/CSV exports for handoff — it does not replace professional review.
Scope Varies by service — some cover payroll, tax prep, or advisory as add-ons. Cash-basis bookkeeping only. Not payroll, inventory, multi-user controls, or full accrual accounting (yet).

You control the agent

With outsourced bookkeeping, you hand off transaction categorization and monthly closes to people who work on a schedule. When they have questions, the back-and-forth adds delay.

Slashbooks flips that model. The agent sees all of your accounts together and recognizes common vendors, so it connects income and expenses with fewer interruptions. You review unfamiliar transactions in plain English, and the system learns from your decisions.

The agent is the interface — it guides setup, close, review, and export. The numbers come from deterministic software that imports transactions, writes ledger entries, validates balances, and generates reports. The AI never invents totals.

Current books, not lagging reports

A common frustration with outsourced bookkeeping is that reports arrive late — sometimes weeks after the month ends. By then you're already making decisions on stale numbers.

Slashbooks is designed so you can run /books-close at the end of each month, pull new bank activity, auto-post trusted transactions, and resolve anything unfamiliar before the books are final. Ask /books-ask anytime for a read on spending, revenue, or owner draws.

Your accountant stays in the loop

Slashbooks is not trying to replace professional review. It's a local bookkeeping system with an automated workflow on top of fixed, auditable accounting logic.

At tax time, run /books-export. The export flow runs sanity checks first — open review items, reconciliation issues, uncategorized transactions, and other handoff problems — then generates CSV and optional Excel workbook files your accountant can review.

Treat Slashbooks output like any client-provided books: useful, structured, and reviewable, but still subject to professional judgment.

Cash-basis only

Outsourced services sometimes offer broader scope — payroll, tax filing, advisory. Slashbooks is intentionally scoped to simple cash-basis bookkeeping for owner-operated businesses.

Bank and card activity drive the books. Owner review handles judgment calls. Slashbooks generates reports from a local ledger store. That's the trade-off: focused and auditable, not a full-service finance department.

What Slashbooks does not replace

  • Your accountant or tax professional.
  • Payroll processing or payroll tax calculations.
  • Tax advice, legal advice, or audit engagements.
  • Inventory tracking or revenue recognition.
  • Multi-user accounting controls.
  • Full accrual accounting.

Slashbooks is beta software. Use it for cash-basis owner-operated businesses only, review its output, and keep an accountant or tax professional in the loop.
