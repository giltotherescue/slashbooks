Slashbooks vs QuickBooks

A qualitative comparison for straightforward, single-entity USD SaaS and service businesses. Slashbooks supports cash books and opt-in accrual within that scope.

QuickBooks is the default accounting app for small businesses. Slashbooks is built for owners who want an agent to guide their bookkeeping, with inspectable local files or a shared Slashbooks Cloud workspace.

At a glance

QuickBooks Slashbooks
Where your books live Hosted in Intuit's cloud. You access them through QuickBooks. A local company folder with its ledger, evidence, supporting records, and reports; or a shared Slashbooks Cloud workspace.
How you work Click through menus, forms, and reports inside the app. Use the plugin in Claude Cowork, Claude Code, or Codex, or another authorized agent through the CLI or Cloud API. Preview changes, review exceptions, and request reports.
Who does the math QuickBooks calculates totals deterministically inside the product. Deterministic books CLI does the math. The agent is the interface — it never invents totals.
Scope Broad platform: invoicing, payroll add-ons, inventory, accrual accounting, multi-user roles, and more. Cash books and opt-in accrual for straightforward USD SaaS and services: journals, fixed-period recognition, outstanding balances, and settlements.
Data sources Bank feeds, manual entry, and integrations within the QuickBooks ecosystem. BankSync, Stripe, Mercury, CSV, QuickBooks exports, and evidence from other connectors or MCPs your agent can access. The engine validates the proposed accounting treatment.
Accountant handoff Accountant access through QuickBooks or exported reports. Financial and supporting reports, Excel/CSV exports, and optional shared Cloud access. QuickBooks comparison uses the same basis and covered period; there is no sync back.

Choose where the books live

With QuickBooks, your ledger lives on Intuit's servers. You use their product to reach it, and your data stays inside their ecosystem.

For local Slashbooks books, each company gets a directory on your machine with its configuration, SQLite ledger, evidence, review queue, and reports. You manage its backups. Slashbooks Cloud is an optional hosted workspace for authorized people and agents, using the same accounting engine.

Your assistant and the connections you enable see the data needed for their work. Choose their access and keep exports and supporting evidence with the books.

Agent vs click-around app

Traditional accounting apps feel bloated because they serve every kind of business at once. You pay in clutter and complexity for features that have nothing to do with how you operate.

Slashbooks gives you a focused foundation shaped to your business. When you need a particular report, chart, or export, you ask the agent and it produces it on the spot — no hunting through menus.

The agent handles setup, preparation, review, questions, and exports through workflows like /books-close, /books-ask, and /books-export. Explicitly delegated scope controls which changes it can apply. Scheduled preparation can bring you current work and exceptions; it does not automatically finalize a period or execute payments.

Focused cash and accrual accounting

QuickBooks can do accrual accounting, payroll, inventory, invoicing, and enterprise-style controls. That's valuable if you need them.

Cash is the Slashbooks default. Opt-in accrual adds reviewed adjusting journals, linked corrections and reversals, prepaid and deferred schedules for known service periods, and outstanding balances with settlements, credits, and write-offs.

Supporting registers tie back to control accounts. Missing evidence, unposted recognition, or a reconciliation difference remains visible and blocks readiness. An imported bank payment must be linked to its existing obligation or given an explicit current-period treatment.

The scope is a single entity in USD with straightforward SaaS or service activity. Inventory, retail, ecommerce, wholesale, manufacturing, construction, consolidation, and complex revenue contracts are outside it.

Backtest before you migrate

If you're considering leaving QuickBooks, Slashbooks doesn't ask you to trust it on faith. Run /books-backtest to compare Slashbooks output against your QuickBooks exports over a historical period.

The backtest surfaces where numbers match, where they differ, and which differences are judgment calls. It doesn't make Slashbooks magically correct — it gives you and your accountant evidence before you switch.

Keep the established source accounting basis at cutover. Cash migration and historical backtesting use the documented cash reports. Accrual cutover requires a full trial balance, chart of accounts, matching balance sheet and fiscal P&L, plus supporting receivables, payables, schedules, and other carried balances. Differences or missing material mappings block activation.

A midyear accrual opening preserves covered fiscal totals as one summary. Reports identify the imported prefix and the activity after cutover, including review of an initial partial month. They do not manufacture earlier monthly detail from a summary.

Read the accrual cutover contract or follow the bookkeeping guide.

What stays outside Slashbooks

  • Sync back to QuickBooks or Google Sheets.
  • Payroll processing or payroll tax calculations.
  • Inventory, job costing, multi-currency, or consolidation.
  • Sending invoices, collecting payments, or moving money.
  • Complex contract revenue recognition or industry-specific accounting.
  • Indirect tax calculations (VAT/GST/HST/sales tax).
  • Replace your accountant or tax professional.

Slashbooks is beta software. Review its output and keep an accountant in the loop.
